Unify structured labs and unstructured notes — govern every column.

Here the notes are a first-class input, as important as the labs — both land in one governed source the app, dashboards, and Genie all read.

  • A Lakeflow medallion pipeline lands raw feeds in bronze, cleans and types them in silver, and rolls them up to a patient-level gold table.
  • Sources: outpatient eGFR / creatinine / albuminuria, medications, the Epic problem-list status, and — critically — the free-text clinical notes.
  • Unity Catalog governs it all — lineage, primary/foreign keys, column comments, and ABAC masking on PHI (MRN shows as MRN-***-****).

The AI reads the notes for hidden CKD — then fuses that with the labs.

CKD at a normal eGFR needs a damage marker (proteinuria) that often lives only in the notes. This step is where those cases get caught.

  • KDIGO stage from mean outpatient eGFR flags the clear cases — but at a normal eGFR (≥90) the labs can look completely fine.
  • AI Functions (ai_query, Llama 3.3 70B) read every clinical note and infer a CKD-risk level (none → high) from indirect signs — foamy urine, edema, rising creatinine — even though the note never says “CKD.”
  • A fusion model (MLflow, on Model Serving) takes the labs and that notes-risk signal → predicts CKD. Neither alone is enough; together they are.
  • The payoff: 74 patients with normal labs were flagged only because the AI read their notes — a labs-only approach misses every one.

Put the right patients in front of a clinician — with the note as the evidence.

Replaces manual chart review. For a caught-by-notes patient the note is the evidence, so the clinician reads exactly what the AI read before confirming.

  • A Databricks App opens on the “Caught by notes” cohort — patients with normal labs the AI surfaced. Each row shows the AI notes-risk and the fusion CKD-risk score.
  • Open a patient: the physician note is right there to read, with the indicators the AI flagged, next to the eGFR trend and the lab-vs-documented gap.
  • A Notes → CKD analyzer lets a clinician drop in any note (or PDF), get a live AI read + fused prediction, and even tune the definition the AI uses.
  • An embedded Genie answers plain-English questions on the same governed data, so its numbers match the worklist.

Record the clinician's confirmed stage — and route it back to Epic, safely.

Nothing auto-writes. A clinician approves every change, and every action is audited.

  • On Approve, the app writes one row to a governed write-back queue (Lakebase / Delta): patient, confirmed stage, referral flag, reviewer, timestamp.
  • In production, a Databricks Job drains that queue into Epic's problem list via FHIR (a Condition resource) or HL7 through the integration layer.
  • The clinician stays in the loop on both ends; the queue is fully audited in Unity Catalog.
